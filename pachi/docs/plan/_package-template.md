# <Slogan naming the player experience>

## 1. Experience goal
- One sentence, emotion-first: "The player feels ___ when they ___."
- The specific playtest/prototype moment that motivates this (the "single
  specific story" — a clip or a note, not a market claim)
- What question this feature answers ("What question am I trying to answer?")

## 2. Appetite
- The timebox (e.g. "one 6-week cycle" / "2 weeks inside cool-down")
- Mechanic tier — primary / secondary / tertiary — and dependencies
  (which primaries must already be shippable)
- What the circuit breaker means here: if it doesn't land by the end,
  it's cut or re-shaped (and what the game falls back to)

## 3. Solution sketch
- Verbs & player activities (atomic → molecular)
- Core loop: how the verb plugs in; the ~30 seconds of fun it creates
- Three-Cs deltas: control mapping changes? camera? character capability?
- Greybox: blockmesh test level / sample space; design parameters locked
  vs. left open for the team
- Signifiers: how the player perceives the affordance (audio, visual,
  UI feedback) — temp assets only
- Bounds of the toy: what the player can NOT yet do with it

## 4. Rabbit holes
- Technical risks → the patch or the spike that must run before betting
- Fun risks → which toy/prototype already produced the feeling
- Pipeline & interdependency risks (rigs, art lead time, engine, primaries)

## 5. No-gos
- Art bar (blockmesh + temp audio only), content count, platforms,
  narrative scope, accessibility remaps, online, etc.
- The pre-agreed cut list if time runs short

## 6. Playtest & "done" criteria
- Player goal / design goal / emotional beat for this feature
- Landing & legibility checks; who plays it, how many, when (≥7 at cycle end)
- Quality bar at handoff: greybox-playable vs. vertical-slice polish
  (concentric rule: the mechanic's own feel should be finished to
  shippable even when its surroundings stay greybox)
