# What is Pachi?

1. # It’s Pachinko

Pachi captures the fun of playing and winning at pachinko.  Players launch balls that hit jackpots and pay out.  It tries to stay true to the rules of Pachinko, rather than breaking them out of convenience.  The balls are both your play resource and your currency.  Lights go off, bells ding, and winning feels exciting.  Pachinko enthusiasts should be able to play and enjoy it without feeling like it’s breaking out of the genre to be a generic pegs and pins game.

2. # It’s Incremental

The player makes progress by upgrading the payouts and capabilities of their play space, which in turn yields either more balls or more valuable balls.  There are at least 2 layered progression systems.

3. # It’s a Deckbuilder

Upgrades are purchased from a shop, and upgrade options are drawn from a deck of cards.  The player can build their deck by “prestiging”, wherein they reset their inner loop progress and spend prestige points to buy more cards for their deck.  Other in-game mechanics may also give them the option of removing cards from their deck.

4. # It’s Simple

Pachi is a game that will be built on a very short timeline, and in turn doesn’t ask much of the player.   The core audience is people who are tired after work and just want to zone out on a game that doesn’t ask much from them.  The systems are pretty simple and legible, and it relies on the satisfaction of its core mechanics to remain engaging.  It’s trying to be a tight, polished incremental timewaster, not god’s gift to incremental gaming.
